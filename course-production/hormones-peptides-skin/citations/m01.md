**Grading rule.** Grade = certainty the source gives for the specific clinical statement the lesson makes, on the guides' A–D scale. A: consistent large RCTs or approval-grade evidence. B: at least one well-designed, adequately powered RCT or an independent, quality-stratified meta-analysis (either direction). C: small or sponsor-funded RCTs without independent replication, or pooled trials dominated by them. D: animal, in-vitro, mechanistic, case series, manufacturer dossiers. 'context' = history, reviews used for framing, trial registrations without results, analytical or regulatory records (regulatory records are primary for status statements, not graded for efficacy). Where the two guides disagree, the lesson states both and the claim takes the grade the primary evidence supports.

In this physiology module most sources are mechanism (cell, tissue ex vivo, animal) and are graded D for any clinical statement; human observational associations are graded C; established physiology and framing reviews are `context`. The ids are `provenance.citations[]` in `packages/hormones-peptides-skin.json`; the claims are `provenance.claims[]`. Both guides (`hannah-shmouni-2026-are`, `hannah-shmouni-2026-ppg-aug`) are registered by the build; only the Aesthetic & Regenerative Endocrinology guide is cited in this module.

## Findings for the author (send with the final draft)

1. A&RE ch.7 ref 1 — the Hormone Cell Atlas is now in print: Fei L, Huang-Doran I, et al. Science 2026;393(6806):eaeb2672 (2 Jul 2026), PMID 42207862. The guide's 'published online May 28, 2026' matches the PubMed entry date.
2. A&RE ch.7 — the figures '~74% of cell types predicted to produce, and 93% to respond to, at least one hormone' and 'endocrine identity tracks cellular lineage more than tissue of origin' could not be verified from the abstract, the atlas portal or press coverage (full text and supplement paywalled). The course teaches only the abstract- and portal-level facts (379 genes, ~14 million cells and nuclei, 47 tissues). Please confirm these against the paper's text.
3. A&RE ch.7 testing pearl — 'serum 25-OH vitamin D … falls with low UVB exposure and sunscreen use regardless of oral intake': Neale et al. 2019 (Br J Dermatol, systematic review) found little evidence that real-life sunscreen use lowers 25(OH)D (the two randomized field trials, SPF ~16, showed no effect; high-SPF products untested), and dietary or supplemental vitamin D is itself converted to 25(OH)D (Bikle 2014), so 'regardless of oral intake' does not hold. The course teaches the trial evidence and keeps sun-protection advice unchanged.
4. A&RE ch.7 senescence paragraph — 'blunting neighboring cells' responsiveness to estrogen, IGF-1, and retinoids, while estrogen loss at menopause is itself associated with a heavier senescent burden and slower fibroblast turnover [4,6]' and 'correcting a true deficiency works partly by easing the senescent … state': neither cited source addresses this (Slominski & Wortsman 2000; Wang & Dreesen 2018, full text read), and a targeted PubMed search found no human study. The course does not teach these statements.
5. A&RE ch.7 — 'SASP-driven MMPs degrade dermal collagen' is stated as established. The human evidence is that aged dermis has more fibroblast MMP-1 and fragmented collagen (Fisher 2009; Varani 2006) and that senescent cells secrete MMPs in culture (Coppé 2008); that senescent cells are the main source in living skin is plausible, not shown. Suggest 'are thought to'.
6. A&RE ch.17 internal inconsistency — the text and table grade senolytics' human evidence GRADE C, while the 'three things to remember' strip says senolytics have 'no human outcome data (D)'. The underlying studies (open-label pilots of 9 and 14 patients, biomarker endpoints) fit D for any clinical claim; the course says so and adds that no randomized trial with skin-ageing outcomes was found (September 2026).
7. A&RE ch.6 — 'Independent work shows keratinocyte-derived, de novo glucocorticoids actively police local immune tone' (Phan 2021): the functional evidence is from mice (keratinocyte Cyp11b1 deletion). Worth naming the species, as the chapter does elsewhere ('cell and ex-vivo').
8. A&RE ch.5 — 'reduces collagen by roughly 1% per year' (ref 1, Shuster 1975): the abstract confirms that skin collagen falls with age and is lower in women but gives no annual rate, and the full text was not accessible. The course does not quote the 1% figure; please confirm the source of the rate.
9. A&RE ch.5 — 'retinoids … remain first-line GRADE A' carries no citation. Suggested primary: Sitohang et al. 2022 (systematic review of 7 RCTs of topical tretinoin for photoageing, PMID 35620028); for naturally aged skin, Kafi et al. 2007 (RCT, PMID 17515510).
10. Not in either guide, worth adding — Ressler 2006 and Waaijer 2016 (senescent cells in human skin, and their link with wrinkles and perceived age), Fisher 2009 and Varani 2006 (the MMP-1–collagen fragmentation loop in aged human dermis), Coppé 2008 (the SASP primary), Chung 2019 (the one randomized human-skin senomorphic study), Hughes 2013 (sunscreen RCT, photoageing), Neale 2019 (sunscreen and vitamin D), and the atlas portal's own statement that its predictions are 'putative and hypothesis-generating'.
