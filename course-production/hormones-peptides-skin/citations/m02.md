**Grading rule.** Grade = certainty the source gives for the specific clinical statement the lesson makes, on the guides' A–D scale. A: consistent large RCTs or approval-grade evidence. B: at least one well-designed, adequately powered RCT or an independent, quality-stratified meta-analysis (either direction). C: small or sponsor-funded RCTs without independent replication, or pooled trials dominated by them. D: animal, in-vitro, mechanistic, case series, manufacturer dossiers. 'context' = history, reviews used for framing, trial registrations without results, analytical or regulatory records (regulatory records are primary for status statements, not graded for efficacy). Where the two guides disagree, the lesson states both and the claim takes the grade the primary evidence supports.

## Findings for the author (send with the final draft)

1. A&RE ch.9 — 'skin collagen falls … on the order of 30% within the first five years, then roughly 2% per year' (refs 1 and 3, both reviews). The primary biopsy studies it traces to report 1–2% a year tracking years since menopause (Brincat 1985, PMID 3978054; Brincat 1987, PMID 3120067; Affinito 1999, PMID 10656502); none of their abstracts states the 30% figure, which appears in reviews (e.g., Thornton 2013). The course teaches 1–2% a year and names 30% as a review figure. Suggest citing the primary study that reports it, or dropping it.
2. A&RE ch.9 grades the skin effect of MHT GRADE B, citing the NAMS 2022 statement. NAMS itself rates that key point Level II (limited or inconsistent) on the strength of one review. The largest RCT of HT on facial skin (Phillips 2008, n=485, PMID 18625536) and the 4-year KEEPS skin study (Owen 2016, PMID 27393520) found no difference in wrinkles; neither is cited by the guide. The course grades structural effects (thickness, collagen) C and says a visible wrinkle benefit has not been shown in the largest trials. The guide may wish to revisit B.
3. A&RE ch.9 ref 8 (Pivazyan 2023) — pools small, heterogeneous studies including non-randomized ones (I² 64–98%), omits KEEPS, and its own authors say MHT should not be considered an independent treatment for skin ageing. Suggest describing it as low-certainty support.
4. A&RE ch.8 'cortisol face' — the opening description of the 'genuine endocrine entity' leads with moon facies and dorsocervical/supraclavicular fat pads, which the Endocrine Society guideline (Nieman 2008, Table 1) lists among common, less discriminatory features; its best discriminators are easy bruising, facial plethora, proximal myopathy and reddish-purple striae >1 cm. The chapter's clinical pearl already lists the right red flags; suggest the description lead with them.
5. A&RE ch.8 — worth adding the guideline's first step: a drug history for exogenous glucocorticoids, including topical and skin-bleaching creams, herbal tonics and joint or nerve injections (Nieman 2008, 3.1), since iatrogenic Cushing's is the commonest form and the most relevant to aesthetic practice.
6. A&RE inherited-syndromes chapter — the Carney complex entry omits cardiac myxomas, present in 20–40% of patients and responsible for more than half of disease mortality (Correa 2015, PMID 26130139). They are the main reason a lentigines-plus-myxoma pattern is a referral. PRKAR1A '~70%' matches Correa 2015 ('more than 70%').
7. A&RE ch.9 — 'topical estrogen creams … are absorbed systemically' is not stated in the cited review's abstract (Shu & Maibach 2011) and was not verified; the course teaches the US regulatory position instead (21 CFR 310.530: an OTC 'hormone cream' is an unapproved new drug).
8. A&RE ch.9 and ch.14 — FDA's menopausal hormone therapy labeling action (request 10 Nov 2025; first six approved labels 12 Feb 2026: cardiovascular, breast-cancer and probable-dementia statements removed from the boxed warning) is not mentioned. Suggest adding it, date-stamped, because it changes how the risk conversation is framed; approved indications did not change and do not include skin.
9. A&RE algorithms pp.93–96 are images only; the course used them to locate claims and sourced every teaching statement to a primary or guideline record. The menopause algorithm's 'topical estrogen off-label (C)' box would read more safely as 'not recommended for wrinkles' to match ch.9's text.
10. All other A&RE ch.8, ch.9 and inherited-syndrome references resolved on PubMed with matching title, first author, journal and year (Slominski 2000, Tiganescu 2011, Schoepe 2006, Nieman 2008, Hall 2005, Thornton 2013, Brincat 2005, NAMS 2022 and 2020, Shu 2011, Manson 2013, Pivazyan 2023, Lause 2017, Darling 1997, Stanescu 2024, Kirschner 2000).
11. Not in either guide, worth adding — Ajjan 2022 (first human RCT of 11β-HSD1 inhibition in skin; pilot, primary outcome missed), Phillips 2008 and Owen 2016 (negative HT wrinkle trials), Martin 2018 (Endocrine Society hirsutism guideline: rapid progression or virilization → androgen-secreting tumour, ~0.2%, over half malignant), Bornstein 2016 (hyperpigmentation distribution in primary adrenal insufficiency), Correa 2015 (Carney complex cardiac myxomas), Hughes 2013 (sunscreen RCT, n=903) and the Renova label (the one FDA-approved adjunct for fine facial wrinkles).
