### Grading rule (applies to every module)

Grade = certainty the source gives for the specific clinical statement the lesson makes, on the guides' A–D scale. A: consistent large RCTs or approval-grade evidence. B: at least one well-designed, adequately powered RCT or an independent, quality-stratified meta-analysis (either direction). C: small or sponsor-funded RCTs without independent replication, or pooled trials dominated by them. D: animal, in-vitro, mechanistic, case series, manufacturer dossiers. 'context' = history, reviews used for framing, trial registrations without results, analytical or regulatory records (regulatory records are primary for status statements, not graded for efficacy). Where the two guides disagree, the lesson states both and the claim takes the grade the primary evidence supports. Observational studies (added 2026-09-24, GRADE starting point; confirmed by Fady Hannah-Shmouni, MD FRCPC, 2026-09-24): adequately sized cohort, case-control or cross-sectional studies grade C for the association they measured; small or single-centre ones grade D; any causal claim drawn from observational data grades D. Meta-analyses of observational studies grade at most B, and only for association. Guidelines and consensus statements are 'context', cited for what they recommend, not graded as evidence.

## Findings for the author (all modules)

1. **m01** — A&RE ch.7 ref 1 — the Hormone Cell Atlas is now in print: Fei L, Huang-Doran I, et al. Science 2026;393(6806):eaeb2672 (2 Jul 2026), PMID 42207862. The guide's 'published online May 28, 2026' matches the PubMed entry date.
2. **m01** — A&RE ch.7 — the figures '~74% of cell types predicted to produce, and 93% to respond to, at least one hormone' and 'endocrine identity tracks cellular lineage more than tissue of origin' could not be verified from the abstract, the atlas portal or press coverage (full text and supplement paywalled). The course teaches only the abstract- and portal-level facts (379 genes, ~14 million cells and nuclei, 47 tissues). Please confirm these against the paper's text.
3. **m01** — A&RE ch.7 testing pearl — 'serum 25-OH vitamin D … falls with low UVB exposure and sunscreen use regardless of oral intake': Neale et al. 2019 (Br J Dermatol, systematic review) found little evidence that real-life sunscreen use lowers 25(OH)D (the two randomized field trials, SPF ~16, showed no effect; high-SPF products untested), and dietary or supplemental vitamin D is itself converted to 25(OH)D (Bikle 2014), so 'regardless of oral intake' does not hold. The course teaches the trial evidence and keeps sun-protection advice unchanged.
4. **m01** — A&RE ch.7 senescence paragraph — 'blunting neighboring cells' responsiveness to estrogen, IGF-1, and retinoids, while estrogen loss at menopause is itself associated with a heavier senescent burden and slower fibroblast turnover [4,6]' and 'correcting a true deficiency works partly by easing the senescent … state': neither cited source addresses this (Slominski & Wortsman 2000; Wang & Dreesen 2018, full text read), and a targeted PubMed search found no human study. The course does not teach these statements.
5. **m01** — A&RE ch.7 — 'SASP-driven MMPs degrade dermal collagen' is stated as established. The human evidence is that aged dermis has more fibroblast MMP-1 and fragmented collagen (Fisher 2009; Varani 2006) and that senescent cells secrete MMPs in culture (Coppé 2008); that senescent cells are the main source in living skin is plausible, not shown. Suggest 'are thought to'.
6. **m01** — A&RE ch.17 internal inconsistency — the text and table grade senolytics' human evidence GRADE C, while the 'three things to remember' strip says senolytics have 'no human outcome data (D)'. The underlying studies (open-label pilots of 9 and 14 patients, biomarker endpoints) fit D for any clinical claim; the course says so and adds that no adequately sized trial with skin-ageing outcomes was found (September 2026); the one exploratory topical-rapamycin RCT described clinical change only qualitatively.
7. **m01** — A&RE ch.6 — 'Independent work shows keratinocyte-derived, de novo glucocorticoids actively police local immune tone' (Phan 2021): the functional evidence is from mice (keratinocyte Cyp11b1 deletion). Worth naming the species, as the chapter does elsewhere ('cell and ex-vivo').
8. **m01** — A&RE ch.5 — 'reduces collagen by roughly 1% per year' (ref 1, Shuster 1975): the abstract confirms that skin collagen falls with age and is lower in women but gives no annual rate, and the full text was not accessible. The course does not quote the 1% figure; please confirm the source of the rate.
9. **m01** — A&RE ch.5 — 'retinoids … remain first-line GRADE A' carries no citation. Suggested primary: Sitohang et al. 2022 (systematic review of 7 RCTs of topical tretinoin for photoageing, PMID 35620028); for naturally aged skin, Kafi et al. 2007 (RCT, PMID 17515510).
10. **m01** — Not in either guide, worth adding — Ressler 2006 and Waaijer 2016 (senescent cells in human skin, and their link with wrinkles and perceived age), Fisher 2009 and Varani 2006 (the MMP-1–collagen fragmentation loop in aged human dermis), Coppé 2008 (the SASP primary), Chung 2019 (the one randomized human-skin senomorphic study), Hughes 2013 (sunscreen RCT, photoageing), Neale 2019 (sunscreen and vitamin D), and the atlas portal's own statement that its predictions are 'putative and hypothesis-generating'.
11. **m01** — Baker 2016 (2026-09-24, from the claim-support full-text read): clearing p16-positive cells during wound healing slowed wound closure in mice (Extended Data Fig. 10), consistent with transient senescence aiding repair (Haykal 2026). This is worth a line in A&RE ch.13/17, where senolytics are discussed for skin.
12. **m02** — A&RE ch.9 — 'skin collagen falls … on the order of 30% within the first five years, then roughly 2% per year' (refs 1 and 3, both reviews). The primary biopsy studies it traces to report 1–2% a year tracking years since menopause (Brincat 1985, PMID 3978054; Brincat 1987, PMID 3120067; Affinito 1999, PMID 10656502); none of their abstracts states the 30% figure, which appears in reviews (e.g., Thornton 2013). The course teaches 1–2% a year and names 30% as a review figure. Suggest citing the primary study that reports it, or dropping it.
13. **m02** — A&RE ch.9 grades the skin effect of MHT GRADE B, citing the NAMS 2022 statement. NAMS itself rates that key point Level II (limited or inconsistent) on the strength of one review. The largest RCT of HT on facial skin (Phillips 2008, n=485, PMID 18625536) and the 4-year KEEPS skin study (Owen 2016, PMID 27393520) found no difference in wrinkles; neither is cited by the guide. The course grades structural effects (thickness, collagen) C and says a visible wrinkle benefit has not been shown in the largest trials. The guide may wish to revisit B.
14. **m02** — A&RE ch.9 ref 8 (Pivazyan 2023) — pools small, heterogeneous studies including non-randomized ones (I² 64–98%), omits KEEPS, and its own authors say MHT should not be considered an independent treatment for skin ageing. Suggest describing it as low-certainty support.

### 03 / 19 · Four units, one circuit.  
*Lesson 1.1 · Concept · audio*

Recent reviews describe the skin as a neuro-immuno-endocrine organ built from four units: epidermal, dermal, hypodermal and adnexal (hair follicles and glands). Each holds cells that can make hormones and mount an immune response. They signal to neighbouring cells, through sensory nerves, and through the blood. Most of this picture comes from cell, tissue and animal studies.

- Four units: epidermal, dermal, hypodermal, adnexal
- Three routes: neighbour to neighbour, nerve, bloodstream
- Mechanism register: cell, tissue and animal work

## Lesson 1.3 · Senescence, the SASP and the ageing dermis

### 13 / 19 · Cells that stop dividing and keep talking.  
*Lesson 1.3 · Concept · audio*

A senescent cell has stopped dividing for good but stays metabolically active, releasing cytokines, matrix-degrading enzymes and growth factors: the senescence-associated secretory phenotype, or SASP. The SASP was characterised in cultured cells. In human skin, cells carrying the senescence marker p16INK4a become more common with age in both epidermis and dermis.

- Senescent: permanent arrest, still secreting
- SASP (IL-6, IL-8, MMPs, growth factors): defined in culture
- Human skin: p16-positive cells rise with age

<details><summary>Narration script</summary>

Senescence begins as a safety mechanism. A cell that has taken enough damage stops dividing for good, which helps guard against cancer and is useful during wound repair. The problem is what the cell does next. It stays alive and metabolically busy and releases a mixture of inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin six and eight, enzymes that break down matrix, and growth factors. This secretory profile, the SASP, was characterised in human cells grown in the laboratory after DNA damage. In living human skin, the evidence is observational. Skin samples from older people contain more cells carrying the senescence marker p16, in both the epidermis and the dermis, and fibroblasts, keratinocytes and melanocytes can all become senescent. Researchers also caution that no single marker identifies a senescent cell with certainty, so counts depend on the marker chosen.

</details>

### 02 / 19 · The skin regenerates its own cortisol.  
*Mechanism · audio*

Skin expresses the machinery of a local stress axis, including CRH and POMC with its product ACTH. In human skin samples its activity is higher with age and in sun-exposed skin, and local cortisol availability is set independently of blood levels. Cortisol slows collagen production in dermal fibroblasts, and mice lacking the enzyme kept their dermal thickness with age.

- Local axis: CRH, POMC and ACTH are made in skin
- 11β-HSD1 regenerates cortisol inside skin cells
- Mechanism register: human samples, cultured cells and mice

In human skin samples that enzyme works harder with age and in sun-exposed skin, and it sets local cortisol independently of the level in the blood. ### 05 / 19 · Rare, and usually from a prescription.  
*The evidence · stat*

Endogenous Cushing's syndrome is uncommon: European population studies put it at two to three new cases per million people a year. The commonest cause of Cushing's syndrome is iatrogenic, from glucocorticoids given as medicine. The Endocrine Society therefore asks for a drug history first, covering tablets, inhalers, injections and creams, including skin-bleaching products, and recommends testing when features are multiple and progressive.

**2–3** — new cases of endogenous Cushing's syndrome per million people per year (European population studies) (As cited in the Endocrine Society diagnostic guideline, 2008)

*Does not mean:* It does not mean you can dismiss it. The guideline recommends testing when discriminating features are multiple and progressive or unusual for age, and some groups, such as people with poorly controlled diabetes, carry a higher rate.

## Lesson 2.2 · Estrogen, menopause and dermal collagen

### 08 / 19 · Estrogen and the dermis.  
*Lesson 2.2 · Concept · audio*

Skin carries estrogen receptors (ERα, ERβ and the membrane receptor GPER) in both sun-protected and sun-damaged sites. In postmenopausal women, two weeks of topical estradiol raised collagen production in sun-protected hip skin; photoaged facial skin showed no such response. After menopause, small biopsy studies found skin collagen and thickness falling year by year, following years since menopause more closely than age. That is mechanism and natural history. What hormone therapy does to a face is a separate question.

- Receptors: ERα, ERβ and GPER in aged and photoaged skin
- Topical estradiol raises collagen in sun-protected skin only
- Collagen loss follows years since menopause

### 09 / 19 · One to two per cent a year.  
*The evidence · stat*

In small biopsy studies of postmenopausal women, skin collagen content and skin thickness fell by about 1–2% a year, in step with bone mass, and followed years since menopause more than age. Reviews, and the Aesthetic & Regenerative Endocrinology guide, quote a steeper early loss of up to 30% in the first five years. That figure traces to the same small studies, and a check of their abstracts in September 2026 did not find it stated.

**1–2%** — fall in skin collagen content and thickness per year after menopause (small biopsy studies) (Brincat et al., 1987 — in parallel with bone mass)  
*Means:* Estrogen withdrawal is a measurable contributor to dermal thinning, and it follows the menopause clock rather than age alone.  
*Does not mean:* It does not show that hormone therapy restores a younger-looking face. These are small observational studies, mostly of non-facial skin; the treatment trials come next.

### 10 / 19 · Biopsy gains, mirror results.

## Lesson 2.3 · Skin hallmarks of inherited and systemic endocrinopathies

### 13 / 19 · Some syndromes show on the skin first.  
*Lesson 2.3 · Concept · audio*

A handful of inherited endocrine syndromes appear on the skin before the glands declare themselves. In multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 (MEN1), multiple facial angiofibromas and collagenomas are common. In Carney complex, lentigines on the lips, eyelids and canthi, blue naevi and small skin myxomas sit alongside myxomas of the heart and cortisol-secreting adrenal disease. In MEN2A, an itchy, rippled, pigmented plaque between the shoulder blades can precede medullary thyroid cancer.

- MEN1: facial angiofibromas, collagenomas
- Carney complex: lip and eyelid lentigines, myxomas
- MEN2A: lichen amyloidosis between the shoulder blades

<details><summary>Narration script</summary>

Some endocrine syndromes are written on the skin years before the glands give them away. In multiple endocrine neoplasia type one, most patients in one careful series had several facial angiofibromas, the same lesions seen in tuberous sclerosis, along with collagenomas on the trunk. In Carney complex, usually caused by inactivating variants in the PRKAR1A gene, freckle-like lentigines cluster on the lips, eyelids and the corners of the eyes, alongside blue naevi and small skin myxomas. The same patients can develop myxomas in the heart and cortisol-secreting adrenal disease. In MEN two A, an itchy, rippled, darkened patch between the shoulder blades, called cutaneous lichen amyloidosis, can appear long before medullary thyroid cancer. Each of these is a referral finding, and none of them is yours to diagnose alone.

</details>

### 14 / 19 · Eighty-eight per cent.  
*The evidence · stat*

In a consecutive series of 32 people with MEN1 examined at the US National Institutes of Health, 88% had multiple facial angiofibromas and 72% had collagenomas. The angiofibromas were clinically and histologically identical to those of tuberous sclerosis. The authors concluded that angiofibromas in someone without tuberous sclerosis call for biochemical testing for MEN1.

**88%** — of 32 people with MEN1 had multiple facial angiofibromas (Darling et al., 1997 — collagenomas in 72%)  
*Means:* Multiple facial angiofibromas are common in MEN1 and can be its first visible sign; in someone without tuberous sclerosis they are a reason for endocrine testing, whatever the cosmetic plan.  
*Does not mean:* It does not mean a single facial papule is MEN1. This is one small referral-centre series, and angiofibromas have other causes, tuberous sclerosis above all.

### 02 / 20 · The skin keeps its own fat.  
*Mechanism · audio*

Beneath the dermis lies subcutaneous fat. Within the reticular dermis itself sits a thinner layer, dermal white adipose tissue. In mice, this layer expands rapidly after a Staphylococcus aureus infection, its new fat cells release the antimicrobial peptide cathelicidin, and the capacity fades with age through TGF-β signalling. Human skin arranges this fat differently, in cones around hair follicles, and human acne lesions contain perifollicular preadipocytes that make cathelicidin. What the layer contributes to human skin ageing has not been measured.

- A second fat layer inside the dermis
- In mice: antimicrobial, and lost with age
- In people: a different shape, and an open question about ageing

<details><summary>Narration script</summary>

Most people picture skin fat as one layer under the dermis. There is a second, thinner layer inside the reticular dermis itself, called dermal white adipose tissue. In mice, this layer expands rapidly after a Staphylococcus aureus infection, and its new fat cells release cathelicidin, an antimicrobial peptide. Older mice lose much of that capacity, through TGF-beta signalling. Human skin arranges this fat differently, in small cones around hair follicles, and human acne lesions contain preadipocytes around the follicle that make cathelicidin. What the layer contributes to human skin ageing has not yet been measured. So hold two things apart when you hear about skin fat. The defence story is strong in mice and emerging in people. The ageing story in people is still a proposal.

</details>

### 03 / 20 · Fat that holds the face up.  
*Human evidence · stat*

Cadaver dissection shows that the face's subcutaneous fat sits in separate compartments, and facial ageing is partly how each compartment changes. Weight shows the same thing from the outside. In a study of 186 pairs of identical twins, the twin with the higher body-mass index looked older before about age 40 and younger after it.

**186** — pairs of identical twins compared for facial ageing (Guyuron et al., 2009 — a four-point higher BMI tracked with an older look before about 40 and a younger look after it)  
*Means:* From midlife on, facial fat goes with a younger-looking face. That fits the anatomy: the face ages partly as its fat compartments change. GRADE C, observational.  
*Does not mean:* It is an observational comparison of photographs. It does not show that gaining weight rejuvenates, or that fat loss on a drug changes the skin itself.

### 04 / 20 · Sugar that sticks to collagen.  
*Mechanism · audio*

Glycation is a slow, non-enzymatic reaction. Reducing sugars attach to protein amino groups, rearrange into Amadori products and end as advanced glycation end products, or AGEs. On long-lived dermal collagen, AGEs form cross-links that stiffen the matrix. Through their receptor, RAGE, AGEs switch on inflammatory signalling in laboratory studies. The Aesthetic & Regenerative Endocrinology guide rates the structural and inflammatory effects B; the course grades the causal link to visible ageing D, because the human data are associations.

- Sugar + protein → Schiff base → Amadori product → AGE
- Cross-links stiffen long-lived collagen
- RAGE signalling: shown in the laboratory

<details><summary>Narration script</summary>

Glycation is chemistry before it is biology. Reducing sugars attach to the amino groups of proteins, rearrange into more stable products, and end as advanced glycation end products, known as AGEs. Dermal collagen lasts for years, so it collects AGEs, and they form cross-links that make the matrix stiffer. AGEs also bind a receptor called RAGE, which in laboratory studies switches on inflammatory signalling and enzymes that break down matrix. Keep the registers apart here. The chemistry, and the build-up of AGEs in human skin, are well documented. The idea that AGEs cause the wrinkles and laxity you see in clinic rests on laboratory and animal work plus human associations. The Aesthetic and Regenerative Endocrinology guide rates the structural and inflammatory effects grade B. This course grades the causal link D, and the next moment shows why.

</details>

### 05 / 20 · Fifteen years on the same collagen.  
*Human evidence · stat*

Human skin collagen turns over slowly; tissue analysis puts its half-life at about 15 years. In skin biopsies, glycoxidation products in collagen rose about five-fold between ages 20 and 85, and were up to twice as high in type 1 diabetes. Skin autofluorescence, a non-invasive reading, correlates moderately with those biopsy measures and was validated in non-pigmented skin.

**~15 years** — estimated half-life of human skin collagen (Verzijl et al., 2000 · AGE content of skin collagen rises about five-fold from age 20 to 85 (Dyer et al., 1993))  
*Means:* Glycation damage accumulates on the same collagen for years, and faster with higher glucose exposure. That much is measured in human skin: direct, observational, GRADE C.  
*Does not mean:* It does not show how much of visible ageing AGEs cause, or that any product reverses established cross-links. Skin autofluorescence is a surrogate reading of AGE load.

## Lesson 3.2 · Incretin therapy and the changing face

### 08 / 20 · Two gut hormones, re-engineered.  
*Lesson 3.2 · Concept · audio*

GLP-1 and GIP are incretins, hormones the gut releases after a meal that amplify glucose-dependent insulin release; GLP-1 also slows gastric emptying and reduces appetite. Native GLP-1 is broken down within minutes. Semaglutide is a long-acting GLP-1 receptor agonist, and tirzepatide activates both GIP and GLP-1 receptors. In the US, as of September 2026, both are FDA-approved drugs for defined indications, set out in the next moment. In the UK both are licensed by the MHRA, and tirzepatide's diabetes and weight-management uses share one brand, Mounjaro.

- Incretins amplify insulin after meals
- Semaglutide: GLP-1 receptor agonist
- Tirzepatide: GIP and GLP-1 receptor agonist

<details><summary>Narration script</summary>

After a meal, the gut releases two hormones called incretins, GLP-1 and GIP. Both amplify insulin release when glucose is high, and GLP-1 also slows the stomach and reduces appetite. Native GLP-1 lasts only minutes in the blood before an enzyme breaks it down, so the drug molecules are engineered to last far longer. Semaglutide activates the GLP-1 receptor. Tirzepatide activates both the GIP and the GLP-1 receptors. Those are the mechanisms. The regulatory facts sit in a separate register. In the United States, as of September 2026, both molecules are FDA-approved drugs, sold under one brand name for type 2 diabetes and another for chronic weight management, with further indications for the heart, the liver and sleep apnoea. In the United Kingdom both are licensed by the MHRA, and tirzepatide's weight-management licence sits on the same brand as its diabetes licence, Mounjaro. The face appears nowhere on those labels, and that is where this lesson begins.

</details>

### 10 / 20 · Large trials, measured on the scale.  
*Human evidence · stat*

In STEP-1, 1,961 adults without diabetes lost a mean 14.9% of body weight on semaglutide over 68 weeks, against 2.4% on placebo. In SURMOUNT-1, 2,539 adults lost 15.0% to 20.9% on tirzepatide over 72 weeks, depending on dose, against 3.1% on placebo. Both Hormonaly guides grade weight management A. Across 35 randomized trials of incretin drugs, a median of about 28% of the weight lost was muscle-related tissue.

**14.9% · 20.9%** — mean weight loss in STEP-1 (semaglutide, 68 weeks) and SURMOUNT-1 (tirzepatide, highest dose, 72 weeks) (Placebo 2.4% and 3.1% · both trials industry-funded, both the basis for approval)  
*Means:* These drugs produce large, reproducible weight loss in randomized trials: GRADE A for the approved indications. Part of what is lost is lean tissue, which is why the Hormonaly guide pairs treatment with protein and resistance training.  
*Does not mean:* Neither pivotal trial measured facial fat, skin laxity or appearance. An A for weight loss says nothing about the face.

## Lesson 3.3 · Gut barrier, endotoxaemia and inflammaging

### 15 / 20 · Inflammation that comes with age.  
*Lesson 3.3 · Concept · audio*

Inflammaging is the chronic, low-grade, sterile inflammation that rises with age. In older adults, higher IL-6 and CRP predict frailty and death. One proposed source is the gut. In mice, a high-fat diet raises circulating bacterial lipopolysaccharide, and infusing it reproduces weight gain and insulin resistance. In 12 healthy men, a single high-fat meal raised plasma endotoxin within four hours. Whether a leaky gut drives skin disease in people has not been shown.

- Inflammaging: low-grade, sterile, age-related
- Metabolic endotoxaemia: gut bacterial LPS in the blood
- Gut to skin in people: not shown

<details><summary>Narration script</summary>

Ageing brings a slow rise in background inflammation, called inflammaging. It is low-grade and sterile, and in older adults higher IL-6 and C-reactive protein predict frailty, disability and death. One proposed source is the gut. In mice, a high-fat diet raises bacterial lipopolysaccharide in the blood, and infusing that endotoxin reproduces weight gain and insulin resistance. In twelve healthy men, a single high-fat meal raised plasma endotoxin within four hours. That is the mechanism usually called metabolic endotoxaemia, or leaky gut. The human evidence stops short of the skin. Measuring gut permeability is difficult, and a leading review concludes that it is still unproven whether restoring the barrier improves disease outside the gut. Hold that line as you look at what patients are sold.

</details>

### 16 / 20 · Thirty-nine trials of probiotics for eczema.  
*The evidence · stat*

An independent Cochrane review pooled 39 randomized trials, with 2,599 participants, of oral probiotics for eczema. Probiotics probably make little or no difference to patient-rated symptoms, and investigator-rated severity fell by less than the minimum clinically important difference. A 2024 European task-force meta-analysis in children without food allergy reported a significant fall in severity scores.

**39 RCTs** — in the Cochrane review of oral probiotics for treating eczema (2,599 participants · little or no difference in patient-rated symptoms (moderate-certainty evidence))  
*Means:* The most direct test of 'treat the gut, help the skin' in a common inflammatory skin disease shows, at most, a small effect. GRADE B for little or no benefit a patient would notice.  
*Does not mean:* It does not rule out benefit from particular strains or in particular groups, and it says nothing about skin ageing. Newer pooled analyses in children report small improvements.

### 18 / 20 · Diet first, a bottle last.  
*What changes in practice · audio*

### 01 / 19 · A spray for the scalp, a tan in a vial.

### 02 / 19 · A follicle that runs its own hormones.  
*Mechanism · audio*

Human scalp hair follicles kept alive in the laboratory answer corticotropin-releasing hormone by making ACTH and α-MSH and secreting cortisol, with a feedback loop like the adrenal axis. They also run a growth hormone and IGF-1 loop of their own. Locally made dihydrotestosterone shrinks genetically susceptible scalp follicles, the process behind pattern hair loss. Growing follicles are shielded from immune attack, and alopecia areata begins when that shield fails.

- Local stress axis: CRH → ACTH → cortisol, with feedback
- Local androgen switch: testosterone → DHT in the scalp
- Immune privilege: its collapse drives alopecia areata

<details><summary>Narration script</summary>

Each hair follicle behaves like a small gland. When researchers keep human scalp follicles alive in culture, the follicles answer corticotropin-releasing hormone the way the pituitary and adrenal glands do. They make ACTH and alpha-MSH, they secrete cortisol, and cortisol-like signals feed back to switch the stimulus down. The same follicles run a growth hormone and IGF-1 loop of their own. On the scalp, an enzyme called five-alpha-reductase turns testosterone into dihydrotestosterone, and in genetically susceptible follicles that hormone drives the gradual shrinking behind pattern hair loss. Growing follicles also hide from the immune system. When that protection collapses, immune cells attack the hair bulb, and the result is alopecia areata. Most of this biology comes from tissue kept alive outside the body, so hold it as mechanism, a reason something might matter in patients.

</details>

### 03 / 19 · Two trials, 1,200 adults, one approval.  
*Human evidence · stat*

When immune privilege fails, the attack depends on cytokine signals that JAK inhibitors interrupt. Two randomized phase 3 trials tested oral baricitinib in 1,200 adults with severe alopecia areata. In the US, FDA approved baricitinib for adults with severe alopecia areata on 13 June 2022. The Aesthetic & Regenerative Endocrinology guide grades it A. In the UK, baricitinib is licensed for severe alopecia areata in adults, and NICE did not recommend it for NHS use in October 2023, on cost-effectiveness.

**1,200** — adults with severe alopecia areata in the two phase 3 trials of baricitinib (2022) (At 36 weeks, up to 39% of patients on baricitinib had at least 80% of their scalp hair, against 3–6% on placebo)  
*Means:* Interrupting the immune signalling behind collapsed privilege regrows hair in severe alopecia areata. That is approval-grade evidence (A), and it shows what real follicle evidence looks like.  
*Does not mean:* It says nothing about pattern hair loss, cosmetic thinning or any peptide. Both trials were funded by the manufacturer, and the authors called for longer trials.

## Lesson 5.2 · Melanocortins: approved afamelanotide and gray-market Melanotan II

### 07 / 19 · Five receptors, one family of hormones.  
*Lesson 5.2 · Concept · audio*

Skin makes proopiomelanocortin (POMC) and cuts it into ACTH, α-MSH and β-endorphin, and the same cells carry the receptors that answer them. Those peptides act on five melanocortin receptors. MC1R drives pigment in skin and hair, MC2R answers ACTH in the adrenal gland, MC3R and MC4R act in the brain on appetite, and MC5R governs sebum. Which receptors a drug reaches decides what else it does.

- MC1R: pigment of skin and hair
- MC2R: adrenal · MC5R: sebum
- MC3R and MC4R: appetite, in the brain

<details><summary>Narration script</summary>

Pigment starts with hormones the skin makes for itself. Skin cells produce a precursor called POMC and cut it into ACTH, alpha-MSH and beta-endorphin, and the same cells carry the receptors that answer them. There are five melanocortin receptors. MC1R sets the pigment of skin and hair. MC2R is the adrenal receptor for ACTH. MC3R and MC4R work in the brain on appetite, and central receptors also drive sexual responses. MC5R governs sebum. Researchers caution that the real map is messier, with receptors turning up in more tissues than the textbook suggests. For this lesson one idea matters most. A drug that stays on MC1R mostly changes pigment, and a drug that reaches the brain's receptors brings other effects with it.

</details>

*The evidence · stat*

*Does not mean:* It says nothing about cosmetic tanning, about Melanotan II, or about unsupervised injections. Even in EPP, the US label recommends a full-body skin examination twice a year.

### 10 / 19 · What the case reports show.  
*Safety signals · audio*

## Lesson 5.3 · Investigational hair peptides and how to grade them

### 14 / 19 · A Wnt switch and a zinc question.  
*Lesson 5.3 · Concept · audio*

PTD-DBM joins a cell-penetrating peptide to a short decoy sequence that stops the protein CXXC5 from binding Dishevelled. Freed from that brake, Wnt/β-catenin signalling rises, and in mice hair regrew faster. Its developers also found CXXC5 raised in human balding scalp. Zinc thymulin is a thymic peptide that is active only when bound to zinc. How it might act on hair has never been defined, and whether any effect would come from the peptide or the zinc is unknown.

- PTD-DBM: releases a Wnt brake (mouse data)
- Zinc thymulin: a zinc-dependent thymic peptide
- Both: mechanism first, human data absent

<details><summary>Narration script</summary>

Two peptides are sold for hair, and both arrive with better stories than data. PTD-DBM joins a cell-penetrating fragment borrowed from an HIV protein to a short decoy sequence. Inside follicle cells, the decoy keeps a protein called CXXC5 from binding its partner, Dishevelled, and that releases a brake on Wnt signalling, a pathway involved in hair regrowth. In mice, hair regrew faster and follicles formed in healed wounds. The work came from the developer's own group. Zinc thymulin is a thymic hormone that becomes active only when zinc binds to it. How it might help hair has never been defined, and nobody has shown whether any effect would come from the peptide or from the zinc. Hold both as mechanism, and look for the human trial.

</details>

<sub>Sources: Lee SH 2017 · Dardenne M 1982 · Mehta A 2025 · The Peptide Pocket Guide — 2026 Edition · Clinical Reference (seventy-two entries) 2026</sub>

## Lesson 6.1 · Growth hormone and IGF-1 in skin homeostasis

### 01 / 19 · Real endocrinology, borrowed for marketing.  
*Module 06 · The growth-hormone axis and the skin · title*

Growth hormone and IGF-1 genuinely shape skin, and one peptide on this axis has approval-grade trials. Most of what patients are offered has much less behind it. In three lessons you will separate the physiology from the promise, the approved from the anecdotal, and the side-effects you can see from the risks nobody has measured.

- 6.1 Growth hormone and IGF-1 in the skin
- 6.2 GHRH analogues and secretagogues: what stands behind each
- 6.3 Signs of excess, oedema and mitogenic risk

### 02 / 19 · Two hormones, two layers of skin.  
*Lesson 6.1 · Mechanism · audio*

Growth hormone acts mostly in the dermis. Its receptor is expressed more by fibroblasts than by keratinocytes, and it increases fibroblast numbers and supports collagen synthesis. IGF-1, made locally by fibroblasts and melanocytes, reaches keratinocytes through the IGF-1 receptor and makes them divide. Human hair follicles even run their own small growth-hormone loop in the laboratory. With age, GH secretion and IGF-1 fall steadily; this is the somatopause.

- GH: dermal fibroblasts and collagen
- IGF-1: keratinocyte proliferation, supplied locally
- Somatopause: GH and IGF-1 fall with age

<details><summary>Narration script</summary>

Two layers of skin listen to two different parts of the same axis. Growth hormone speaks mainly to the dermis. Its receptor is expressed more on fibroblasts than on keratinocytes, and in culture growth hormone increases fibroblast numbers and supports collagen synthesis, sometimes directly and sometimes through IGF-1. The epidermis listens to IGF-1. Keratinocytes carry the IGF-1 receptor, their neighbours in the dermis supply the hormone, and in culture IGF-1 makes keratinocytes divide where growth hormone on its own does nothing. Human hair follicles even keep a small growth-hormone loop running when they are cultured outside the body. With age, growth-hormone pulses and IGF-1 fall steadily, a change called the somatopause. Hold all of this as mechanism. It explains why the axis could matter for skin. Whether changing it helps a patient is a separate question, and the trials answer it.

</details>

<sub>Sources: Tavakkol A 1992 · Edmondson SR 2003 · Horesh EJ 2023 · Veldhuis JD 2008 · Aesthetic & Regenerative Endocrinology — A Clinician's Pocket Guide 2026</sub>

### 03 / 19 · A much-cited skin result, read again.  
*Human evidence · stat*

In 1990 a much-cited New England Journal of Medicine study gave growth hormone for six months to 12 healthy men over 60, with nine untreated men for comparison and no placebo. Lean mass rose and fat fell. Skin thickness rose 7.1%, with P = 0.07, short of statistical significance. A small 1996 randomized trial in 28 older men found 15% more collagen laid down in implanted wound tubes on GH, a laboratory surrogate for repair.

**7.1%** — rise in skin thickness in 12 older men given growth hormone for six months (1990) (P = 0.07 · nine untreated men as comparison · no placebo)  
*Means:* A skin result still quoted for growth hormone comes from 12 treated men, without a placebo, and it missed statistical significance. Graded on its design, it is D.  
*Does not mean:* It does not show that GH thins skin or that it fails in true deficiency, and it says nothing about secretagogues. It shows how thin the direct skin evidence for growth hormone in healthy ageing is.

## Lesson 6.2 · GHRH analogues and secretagogues: clinical pharmacology

### 07 / 19 · One axis, four ways in.  
*Lesson 6.2 · Concept · audio*

Somatropin is growth hormone itself. GHRH analogues (sermorelin, tesamorelin, CJC-1295) stimulate the pituitary's GHRH receptor. Ghrelin-receptor agonists (ipamorelin, GHRP-2, GHRP-6 and the oral ibutamoren, MK-677) stimulate GH release through a second pituitary receptor. IGF-1 analogues such as IGF-1 LR3 bypass the pituitary altogether. They are sold as one family, and the next moments take them apart.

- Replace: somatropin
- Stimulate: GHRH analogues and ghrelin-receptor agonists
- Bypass: IGF-1 analogues

<details><summary>Narration script</summary>

Four routes lead into the same axis, and patients rarely know which one they are on. Somatropin is growth hormone itself, made by recombinant technology. GHRH analogues, sermorelin, tesamorelin and CJC-1295, press on the pituitary's releasing-hormone receptor, so the gland releases its own growth hormone in pulses. Ghrelin-receptor agonists, ipamorelin, GHRP-2, GHRP-6 and the oral compound ibutamoren, press on a second pituitary receptor. IGF-1 analogues such as IGF-1 LR3 skip the pituitary and act on the IGF-1 receptor directly. Every route ends at that same receptor, which is why lesson three treats their risks together. Clinics often sell all of these as one family with one promise. Keep them apart, because the trials, the approvals and the side-effects belong to each molecule separately.

</details>

### 15 / 19 · Does raising IGF-1 cause cancer?  
*Four registers · tool*

### 02 / 22 · GRADE rates certainty in an effect, one outcome at a time.  
*Lesson 7.1 · Concept · audio*

GRADE sorts the evidence on a specific question into four levels of certainty: high, moderate, low and very low. The rating applies to a body of evidence for one outcome, never to a single study or to a molecule. Randomized trials start high and observational studies start low; certainty then falls for risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision or suspected publication bias, and occasionally rises for a very large effect. Both Hormonaly guides print the letters A to D for the same idea, adapted, and attach each letter to a specific use.

- Four levels, applied to a body of evidence
- Five named reasons to rate down
- The letter belongs to a use, never to a molecule

<details><summary>Narration script</summary>

Every claim you hear about a skin peptide carries a hidden question. How sure can we be? GRADE answers it with four levels of certainty, from high to very low. The rating belongs to a body of evidence for one outcome, such as wrinkle depth at twelve weeks, and never to a single study or to the molecule itself. Randomized trials start high and observational studies start low. From there, certainty drops for five reasons you can name, which are risk of bias, inconsistency between trials, indirectness, imprecision, and suspected publication bias. It can occasionally rise, for example when an effect is very large. Both Hormonaly guides print a letter from A to D beside each use. Read those letters as the same four levels, adapted. ## Lesson 7.2 · Compounding law, the 40-amino-acid line and certificates of analysis

### 07 / 22 · Two compounding routes, one strict order.  
*Lesson 7.2 · Concept · audio*

In the US, section 503A lets a licensed pharmacist or physician compound for an identified patient on a prescription, exempt from new-drug approval, adequate directions for use and good manufacturing practice. The bulk substance must meet a USP or NF monograph; failing that, be a component of an approved drug; failing that, appear on FDA's 503A bulks list. It must also come from an FDA-registered establishment with a valid certificate of analysis. Section 503B outsourcing facilities register with FDA, work to good manufacturing practice under inspection, and may compound without patient-specific prescriptions.

- 503A: patient-specific, three exemptions
- Monograph, then approved component, then the bulks list
- 503B: registered, inspected, held to good manufacturing practice

<details><summary>Narration script</summary>

Two routes exist for compounding in the United States, and the difference between them shapes the rest of this lesson. Under section 503A, a licensed pharmacist or physician prepares a drug for an identified patient on a prescription. If the conditions are met, the product escapes three requirements, namely approval as a new drug, labelling with adequate directions, and good manufacturing practice. The conditions start with the substance itself, taken in strict order. A USP or National Formulary monograph comes first. Only when there is none can it be a component of an approved drug, and only when neither applies can it sit on FDA's 503A bulks list. The substance must also come from a registered manufacturer with a valid certificate of analysis. Section 503B outsourcing facilities work differently. They register with FDA, work to good manufacturing practice, face risk-based inspection, and can compound without a prescription for each patient. Their products remain unapproved.

</details>

### 09 / 22 · Forty amino acids.  
*Peptide or biologic · stat*

Since 23 March 2020 FDA defines a protein as an alpha amino acid polymer of more than 40 amino acids, whether or not it is chemically synthesized, so anything above the line is a biological product. On that date tesamorelin, a 44-residue GHRH analogue, and the enzyme hyaluronidase moved to biologics licences, and FDA told compounders that transitioned biologics cannot use the 503A or 503B exemptions.

### 11 / 22 · What a certificate can and cannot prove.  
*Concept · audio*

A certificate of analysis is the supplier's own report on a named lot. Mass spectrometry checks identity, and a careful certificate prints the expected mass beside the observed one. HPLC gives purity as a percentage, which says nothing about what the impurities are. Endotoxin, microbial, water, counter-ion, metal and solvent results complete it. A certificate cannot prove that the vial in your hand came from that lot, how it was stored since, or that it is sterile and suitable to inject.

- Identity: mass spectrometry, observed against expected
- Purity: an HPLC percentage, impurities unnamed
- Suitability to inject needs sterility, endotoxin and the right lot

<details><summary>Narration script</summary>

Picture the certificate a patient hands you, and read it line by line, asking what each line actually tests. Mass spectrometry checks identity, and a careful certificate shows the expected mass next to the one measured. HPLC reports purity as a percentage of the main peak, which tells you how much else is present and nothing about what that something else is. Endotoxin matters for anything injected, and FDA's guidance says the acceptable limit is calculated from the dose and the route, so a bare figure settles little. When FDA reviewed BPC-157 for its advisory committee, most public certificates listed purity alone, and the one a nominator supplied mentioned no endotoxin test. Then remember what paper cannot do. A certificate describes a lot. It cannot show that your vial came from that lot, how the vial was stored, or that it is sterile.

### 17 / 22 · Off-label needs a label.  
*Lesson 7.3 · Concept · audio*

Off-label prescribing means using an approved drug for a use its labelling does not cover, and FDA accepts it as clinical judgment. A never-approved peptide has no label to depart from, so prescribing it is use of an unapproved drug. Introducing an unapproved new drug into interstate commerce is a prohibited act under the FD&C Act, and FDA's 2020 warning letter to a compounding pharmacy named BPC-157 and GHK-Cu on exactly this basis.

- Off-label applies only to approved drugs
- A never-approved peptide has no label
- Compounded is still unapproved

<details><summary>Narration script</summary>

Off-label is the phrase to get right first. FDA uses it for an approved drug prescribed outside its labelling, which prescribers generally may do when they judge it medically appropriate. Under federal law, putting an unapproved new drug into interstate commerce is a prohibited act, and in 2020 FDA warned a compounding pharmacy about its BPC-157 and GHK-Cu products on that basis. So write the real category in the chart, every time.

**hormone cell atlas · cell atlas · single cell atlas · receptor map** (m01, `tutor-m01-atlas`)

The Hormone Cell Atlas (Fei, Huang-Doran et al., Science 2026) read RNA for 379 hormone and receptor genes in about 14 million cells and nuclei from 47 human tissues and predicted which cell types could make or respond to hormones. Its own authors call these predictions putative and hypothesis-generating: RNA shows capacity, not secretion or effect. Treat any 'skin has receptors for it' product claim as a hypothesis until protein, function and a human trial back it. A few skin outputs are established in people, such as vitamin D3 formed under UVB and released into the blood.

Sources: fei-2026-hormone-cell-atlas, hormone-cell-atlas-portal, liu-2016-mrna-protein, holick-1980-previtamin-d3

**senolytic · senolytics · senescence · senescent cells** (m01, `tutor-m01-senescence`)

Senescent cells accumulate in human epidermis and dermis with age, and in one cohort their number tracked with wrinkles and perceived age: an association. Their secretions (the SASP: IL-6, IL-8, matrix-degrading enzymes) were defined in cultured cells. Evidence that clearing senescent cells improves ageing comes from mice; human data are small open-label or exploratory pilots measuring tissue markers. The one randomized study in skin (topical rapamycin, a senomorphic, 2019) was small and exploratory: it lowered p16, and clinical change was only described. No adequately sized trial with clinical skin-ageing outcomes was found (September 2026). In mice, clearing senescent cells during healing slowed wound closure. The Aesthetic & Regenerative Endocrinology guide advises confining senolytics to clinical trials. This course gives no doses or product advice; daily sunscreen and topical retinoids are the levers with human trials, and a prescriber decides on any treatment.

Sources: waaijer-2016-p16-wrinkles, coppe-2008-sasp, baker-2016-p16-clearance-mice, hickson-2019-senolytic-dkd-pilot, chung-2019-topical-rapamycin, hannah-shmouni-2026-are

**cortisol face · moon face · puffy face cortisol · is it cushing's** (m02, `tutor-m02-cortisol-face`)

Most 'cortisol face' is puffiness from sleep, alcohol, salt and weight, as the Aesthetic & Regenerative Endocrinology guide describes it. Endogenous Cushing's syndrome is rare (two to three new cases per million people a year), and its commonest form comes from a glucocorticoid the patient is using, so ask about tablets, inhalers, injections and creams, including skin-lighteners. The features that best discriminate Cushing's are easy bruising, facial plethora, proximal muscle weakness and reddish-purple striae wider than 1 cm; a round face alone is a weak signal. If several are present and progressing, the next step is formal testing arranged by a prescriber and an endocrinologist, not a supplement or a consumer cortisol panel. Local skin cortisol (11β-HSD1) is a research mechanism with no approved treatment.

Sources: nieman-2008-cushings-diagnosis-guideline, tiganescu-2013-11bhsd1-aged-skin, hannah-shmouni-2026-are

**hrt for skin · hormone therapy skin · estrogen for wrinkles · estrogen cream face** (m02, `tutor-m02-hrt-skin`)

Estrogen acts on receptors in skin. In small trials, hormone therapy thickened skin and raised collagen (graded C here). The largest trial on facial skin (485 women, 48 weeks) and a four-year trial in recently menopausal women found no difference in wrinkles. In the US, as of February 2026, approved uses of menopausal hormone therapy are hot flashes, vaginal and vulvar symptoms and, for some products, preventing bone loss; skin is not an indication and is never the reason to start. Compounded 'bioidentical' hormones lack efficacy and safety data, and an over-the-counter 'hormone cream' is an unapproved drug under US rules. In the UK, HRT products are prescription-only medicines licensed for oestrogen-deficiency symptoms and, for some, for preventing osteoporosis; skin is not among their uses, and a compounded 'bioidentical' hormone is an unlicensed medicine that MHRA guidance places behind any licensed product that meets the need. For skin, daily sunscreen and a prescription retinoid have better evidence. This course gives no doses; hormone therapy decisions belong with a prescriber.

**ozempic face · facial fat loss on semaglutide · tirzepatide face changes · glp-1 facial volume loss** (m03, `m03-tutor-ozempic-face`)

'Ozempic face' describes facial hollowing and laxity during weight loss on incretin drugs. Mechanism: losing fat deflates the superficial facial fat compartments and unmasks laxity already present. Human evidence is observational and small: one retrospective imaging study of 20 patients found a median 9% loss of midface volume, mostly superficial, about 7% per 10 kg lost (D), and a survey of 1,226 patients found sagging, facial volume loss and hair shedding reported more often the more weight was lost (C). No randomized trial has measured the face. Regulatory status (US, September 2026): semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy) and tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound) are FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes, chronic weight management and further heart, liver and sleep-apnoea indications. Their labels list hair loss (about 3–6% on drug against about 1% on placebo, mostly in women, linked to weight reduction) but not facial volume loss. In the UK (September 2026), Ozempic is licensed for type 2 diabetes, Wegovy for weight management and cardiovascular risk reduction, and Mounjaro for both type 2 diabetes and weight management; UK product information also lists hair loss, and MHRA says retatrutide is not authorised in the UK. Open question: whether the drugs act directly on facial fat, beyond weight loss, rests on cell studies. In practice: raise facial and hair changes before treatment, record concerns with consented photographs, refer restoration requests to a qualified aesthetic clinician, and take any question about stopping or changing the medicine to the prescriber. The course gives no dosing advice.

**leaky gut · gut skin axis · zonulin test · probiotics for eczema** (m03, `m03-tutor-gut-skin`)

Mechanism: bacterial lipopolysaccharide crossing a leaky gut barrier ('metabolic endotoxaemia') drives inflammation and insulin resistance in mice, and a single high-fat meal briefly raised plasma endotoxin in a study of 12 healthy men (D). Human evidence for skin: an independent Cochrane review of 39 trials found probiotics probably make little or no difference to patient-rated eczema symptoms (B); a 2024 meta-analysis in children without food allergy reported small score improvements (C); one small randomized trial found rosacea improved after small-intestinal bacterial overgrowth was treated (C). A leading review concludes it is unproven that restoring barrier function improves disease outside the gut, and a widely used commercial zonulin assay detects proteins other than zonulin. A fibre-rich diet has strong evidence for cardiometabolic outcomes and has not been tested on skin outcomes. The Hormonaly guide grades the 'leaky gut' repair stack (L-glutamine, zinc carnosine, BPC-157) D; in the US, BPC-157 is not on the 503A bulks list as of September 2026. In the UK, an unlicensed medicine can be supplied as a 'special' only for an individual patient's special clinical need that no licensed product meets, on the prescriber's responsibility. Start with diet, metabolic health and the skin diagnosis; investigational peptides come last, if at all, and any injected or research peptide needs a prescriber.

**ghk-cu · ghk · copper peptide · copper peptides** (m04, `tutor-m04-ghkcu`)

Topical GHK-Cu has a plausible copper-enzyme mechanism and small-trial human support (GRADE C); injected GHK-Cu has no randomized trials and commonly irritates. In the US, as of FDA's 14 May 2026 list, non-injectable GHK-Cu is in 503A Category 1 (under evaluation, not approved); the injectable route is excluded. In the UK, a GHK-Cu serum presented for appearance is regulated as a cosmetic, and cosmetics law excludes anything injected; MHRA decides from its claims, form and use whether an injected product is a medicine, and a medicine with no UK marketing authorisation may not be sold, supplied or advertised. This course gives no doses or sourcing advice — for an individual patient, that is the prescriber's decision.

**bpc-157 · bpc 157 · bpc · tb-500** (m04, `tutor-m04-bpc157`)

BPC-157's evidence is rodent studies from one group plus uncontrolled case series; no randomized efficacy trial in people has been published in full (September 2026); the one randomized trial FDA found, in ulcerative colitis, exists only as a 2005 meeting abstract and showed no significant difference from placebo. TB-500 is a seven-residue fragment of thymosin beta-4 that has never been trialled; KPV has only mouse data. GLOW and KLOW are marketing names for untested blends. FDA's advisory committee reviewed BPC-157, KPV and TB-500 in July 2026 — advice only; they are not on the 503A bulks list. In the UK, a medicine with no marketing authorisation may not be sold, supplied or advertised, and MHRA's largest seizure of unlicensed weight-loss medicines, in May 2026, included peptide products. New pigmentation while using research-use-only peptides is a red flag: stop, record the product and source, and get it assessed.

**melanotan · melanotan ii · melanotan 2 · mt2** (m05, `tutor-m05-melanotan`)

Afamelanotide (Scenesse) is an MC1R agonist approved only for erythropoietic protoporphyria (EU 2014, US 2019; licensed in the UK, where NICE did not recommend it for NHS use in 2023), on the strength of two sponsor-funded phase 3 trials; even there, the US label recommends a full-body skin examination twice a year. Melanotan II is a different, non-selective molecule approved for no indication. Its only controlled human data are two tiny 1990s studies, and case reports describe darkening and new moles, nail pigmentation, melanomas in people with other risk factors (causation unproven), priapism and rhabdomyolysis. FDA (April 2026), MHRA (April 2024) and TGA (August 2026) have all put warnings on record. If someone has used it: stop, record the product and seller, examine the skin and nails, and refer changing moles; a personal or family history of melanoma makes it an absolute no in the endocrinology guide. This course gives no doses or sourcing advice.

**ptd-dbm · ptd dbm · zinc thymulin · thymulin** (m05, `tutor-m05-hair-peptides`)

PTD-DBM releases a brake (CXXC5) on Wnt signalling; its whole evidence is one 2017 mouse and tissue study from the developer's group, with no human trial published or registered as of September 2026 (grade D). Zinc thymulin is a zinc-dependent thymic peptide with no indexed human hair study (grade D). In the US, on FDA's 14 May 2026 lists, thymulin acetate is in 503A Category 3 and PTD-DBM is on no list; neither is on the bulks list. Hair loss starts with a workup (pattern, trigger, patches, androgens; ferritin and TSH in everyone), and the approved options, minoxidil, finasteride in men and baricitinib for severe alopecia areata, carry grade A evidence. In the UK, a men's minoxidil foam is on general sale, finasteride for hair loss is a private prescription carrying MHRA's strengthened warnings on depression and sexual dysfunction (May 2026), baricitinib is licensed for severe alopecia areata, and a peptide spray made up for one patient would be an unlicensed special that MHRA guidance places behind any licensed product that could meet the need. This course gives no doses or sourcing advice; treatment choices belong with the prescriber.

**cjc-1295 · cjc 1295 · ipamorelin · sermorelin** (m06, `tutor-m06-secretagogues`)

Tesamorelin is the one peptide on this axis with approval-grade evidence: two phase 3 trials and US approval (2010) for excess abdominal fat in adults with HIV-associated lipodystrophy, GRADE A for that use only. Sermorelin, CJC-1295 with DAC and ipamorelin have human hormone-level data but no trial showing a clinical or skin benefit; CJC-1295 without DAC and IGF-1 LR3 have no published human studies (this course grades all of these D). In the US, as of FDA's lists of April and May 2026, no growth-hormone secretagogue is on the 503A bulks list; ibutamoren (MK-677) is in Category 2, and the CJC-1295 and ipamorelin 503A nominations were withdrawn. Growth hormone itself is approved for diagnosed deficiency and a few growth disorders, and knowingly distributing it for anti-ageing is a federal offence in the US. In the UK, somatropin is a prescription-only medicine and a Class C controlled drug, and MHRA's products database holds no product information for tesamorelin, sermorelin or ipamorelin (September 2026). This course gives no doses or sourcing advice; for an individual patient, that is a prescriber's decision.

**too much growth hormone · gh excess signs · acromegaly skin · swollen hands** (m06, `tutor-m06-gh-excess`)

Signs of growth-hormone excess include puffy, thickened skin of the face, hands and feet, oily skin, more sweating, skin tags, acanthosis nigricans, tight rings or shoes, night-time tingling and carpal tunnel symptoms. On GH-axis drugs, fluid retention with swelling and joint pain is common and usually settles when treatment stops. The Endocrine Society recommends IGF-1 testing when typical acral and facial features are present, so route these patients to a prescriber or to endocrinology. On cancer, keep four answers apart: the IGF-1 receptor drives cell growth (mechanism); higher natural IGF-1 goes with modestly higher risk of some cancers (observation); GH replacement in true deficiency has not shown a clear risk in large cohorts; and for secretagogues or IGF-1 LR3 used for skin, long-term cancer data do not exist (open question).

**grade · grade a · grade c · evidence grade** (m07, `tutor-m07-grade`)

GRADE rates certainty for a specific outcome across a body of evidence: high, moderate, low or very low (the Hormonaly guides print A to D). Randomized trials start high; certainty drops for risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision or suspected publication bias. Industry-sponsored studies more often report favourable results (Cochrane, 2017), so ask who ran and paid for the trials. A grade is separate from legal status: check both, and never let a mechanism raise a grade. This course gives no doses or sourcing advice.

Sources: balshem-2011-grade-rating-quality, guyatt-2025-core-grade-4-bias, lundh-2017-industry-sponsorship, hannah-shmouni-2026-ppg-aug

**certificate of analysis · coa · hplc · purity** (m07, `tutor-m07-compounding-coa`)

In the US, a 503A pharmacy may compound only from a substance that meets a USP or NF monograph, or else is a component of an approved drug, or else is on the 503A bulks list (six substances, none a peptide, as of September 2026), and it needs a valid certificate of analysis. A certificate is the supplier's report on one lot: mass spectrometry for identity, HPLC for purity, plus endotoxin and microbial results. It cannot prove your vial is that lot, how it was stored, or that it is sterile or lawful. 'Research use only' products are labelled not for use in humans, and 'off-label' applies only to approved drugs. In the UK, an unlicensed medicine reaches a patient as a 'special', made by a licensed specials manufacturer to a prescriber's order for one patient's special clinical need, or as a medicine a pharmacist prepares against a prescription; MHRA guidance ranks a UK-licensed medicine, even used off-label, ahead of an unlicensed one, and specials may not be advertised. This course gives no doses or sourcing advice; for an individual patient, that is the prescriber's decision.
