## Lesson 5.1 · The hair follicle as an endocrine mini-organ

### 01 / 19 · A spray for the scalp, a tan in a vial.  
*Module 05 · Follicle, pigment and melanocortins · title*

### 02 / 19 · A follicle that runs its own hormones.  
*Mechanism · audio*

Human scalp hair follicles kept alive in the laboratory answer corticotropin-releasing hormone by making ACTH and α-MSH and secreting cortisol, with a feedback loop like the adrenal axis. They also run a growth hormone and IGF-1 loop of their own. Locally made dihydrotestosterone shrinks genetically susceptible scalp follicles, the process behind pattern hair loss. Growing follicles are shielded from immune attack, and alopecia areata begins when that shield fails.

- Local stress axis: CRH → ACTH → cortisol, with feedback
- Local androgen switch: testosterone → DHT in the scalp
- Immune privilege: its collapse drives alopecia areata

<details><summary>Narration script</summary>

Each hair follicle behaves like a small gland. When researchers keep human scalp follicles alive in culture, the follicles answer corticotropin-releasing hormone the way the pituitary and adrenal glands do. They make ACTH and alpha-MSH, they secrete cortisol, and cortisol-like signals feed back to switch the stimulus down. The same follicles run a growth hormone and IGF-1 loop of their own. On the scalp, an enzyme called five-alpha-reductase turns testosterone into dihydrotestosterone, and in genetically susceptible follicles that hormone drives the gradual shrinking behind pattern hair loss. Growing follicles also hide from the immune system. When that protection collapses, immune cells attack the hair bulb, and the result is alopecia areata. Most of this biology comes from tissue kept alive outside the body, so hold it as mechanism, a reason something might matter in patients.

</details>

### 03 / 19 · Two trials, 1,200 adults, one approval.  
*Human evidence · stat*

When immune privilege fails, the attack depends on cytokine signals that JAK inhibitors interrupt. Two randomized phase 3 trials tested oral baricitinib in 1,200 adults with severe alopecia areata. In the US, FDA approved baricitinib for adults with severe alopecia areata on 13 June 2022. The Aesthetic & Regenerative Endocrinology guide grades it A.

**1,200** — adults with severe alopecia areata in the two phase 3 trials of baricitinib (2022) (At 36 weeks, up to 39% of patients on baricitinib had at least 80% of their scalp hair, against 3–6% on placebo)  
*Means:* Interrupting the immune signalling behind collapsed privilege regrows hair in severe alopecia areata. That is approval-grade evidence (A), and it shows what real follicle evidence looks like.  
*Does not mean:* It says nothing about pattern hair loss, cosmetic thinning or any peptide. Both trials were funded by the manufacturer, and the authors called for longer trials.

### 04 / 19 · Four questions before any product.  
*Worked tool · tool*

The Aesthetic & Regenerative Endocrinology guide's first-pass hair-loss workup asks four questions in order. Each yes points to a different condition and a different next step.

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| Patterned thinning? (crown, vertex, frontal recession) | Androgenetic alopecia | Topical minoxidil; finasteride in men; treat coexisting iron or thyroid problems |
| Diffuse shedding after a trigger? (illness, delivery, diet, a drug) | Telogen effluvium | Remove the trigger, reassure, recheck ferritin and TSH |
| Patchy, smooth loss? (with or without nail pitting) | Alopecia areata | Dermatology referral; screen for thyroid and autoimmune disease |
| Signs of androgen excess? (hirsutism, acne, irregular periods) | Hyperandrogenism work-up | Follow the guide's acne and androgen pathway |
| In every patient | Ferritin and TSH | Add androgens if there are virilising signs; a scarring pattern needs a biopsy |

**Callout:** Refer severe or rapidly progressive alopecia areata promptly.

### 05 / 19 · Work up the hair loss before anyone mentions a peptide.  
*What changes in practice · audio*

Pattern, trigger, patches and androgens decide the treatment. Pooled randomized trials support topical minoxidil in men and women and finasteride in men. In the US, as of September 2026, topical minoxidil is FDA-approved and sold over the counter, finasteride for pattern hair loss is a prescription drug, and baricitinib is approved for adults with severe alopecia areata.

- Workup first: ferritin and TSH in everyone
- Approved options exist, with A-grade evidence
- Severe or fast-spreading alopecia areata goes to dermatology

**By role**
- *Prescriber:* Complete the workup and record the pattern before prescribing. Offer the approved options with their grades, refer severe or rapidly progressive alopecia areata, and document any investigational product the patient raises and what you advised.
- *Clinical staff:* Note the pattern, the timeline and any recent trigger, photograph the scalp with consent if your setting allows, and pass it to the prescriber. Patchy or fast-spreading loss needs prompt escalation.
- *Advisor:* Explain what the approved options are and what their evidence shows. A patient with new, patchy, scarring or fast-spreading hair loss needs a prescriber before any product.

In the United States minoxidil is sold over the counter, while finasteride for hair loss needs a prescription. ## Lesson 5.2 · Melanocortins: approved afamelanotide and gray-market Melanotan II

### 07 / 19 · Five receptors, one family of hormones.  
*Lesson 5.2 · Concept · audio*

Skin makes proopiomelanocortin (POMC) and cuts it into ACTH, α-MSH and β-endorphin, and the same cells carry the receptors that answer them. Those peptides act on five melanocortin receptors. MC1R drives pigment in skin and hair, MC2R answers ACTH in the adrenal gland, MC3R and MC4R act in the brain on appetite, and MC5R governs sebum. Which receptors a drug reaches decides what else it does.

- MC1R: pigment of skin and hair
- MC2R: adrenal · MC5R: sebum
- MC3R and MC4R: appetite, in the brain

<details><summary>Narration script</summary>

Pigment starts with hormones the skin makes for itself. Skin cells produce a precursor called POMC and cut it into ACTH, alpha-MSH and beta-endorphin, and the same cells carry the receptors that answer them. There are five melanocortin receptors. MC1R sets the pigment of skin and hair. MC2R is the adrenal receptor for ACTH. MC3R and MC4R work in the brain on appetite, and central receptors also drive sexual responses. MC5R governs sebum. Researchers caution that the real map is messier, with receptors turning up in more tissues than the textbook suggests. For this lesson one idea matters most. A drug that stays on MC1R mostly changes pigment, and a drug that reaches the brain's receptors brings other effects with it.

</details>

<sub>Sources: Slominski A 2000 · Laiho L 2022 · Böhm M 2025 · Aesthetic & Regenerative Endocrinology — A Clinician's Pocket Guide 2026</sub>

### 08 / 19 · One receptor family, two very different molecules.  
*Four registers · tool*

Afamelanotide and Melanotan II are both synthetic relatives of α-MSH. Compare them register by register and the resemblance ends at the receptor.

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Afamelanotide: MC1R agonist · Melanotan II: non-selective | Melanotan II also acts on MC3R, MC4R and MC5R and reaches the brain |
| Human evidence | Afamelanotide: A, for EPP only · Melanotan II: D | Two phase 3 trials in erythropoietic protoporphyria, against a three-volunteer pilot and a ten-man erectile-dysfunction study from the 1990s. The Peptide Pocket Guide rates Melanotan II C and the Aesthetic & Regenerative Endocrinology guide D; with no trial of tanning, this course uses D. |
| Regulatory status | Afamelanotide: EU 2014, US 2019 · Melanotan II: none | Afamelanotide is approved only for EPP, as the manufacturer's implant. Melanotan II is approved for no indication anywhere. |
| Open question | Uses beyond EPP; melanoma risk | A phase 3 trial of afamelanotide in vitiligo began in 2023 and has posted no results. Whether Melanotan II causes melanoma is unproven. |

**Callout:** A shared receptor does not bring a shared approval.

### 09 / 19 · Sixty-nine hours against forty-one.  
*The evidence · stat*

Erythropoietic protoporphyria (EPP) is a rare disorder in which sunlight causes acute, severe pain. In the US phase 3 trial, adults on afamelanotide spent a median 69.4 hours in direct sunlight without pain over six months, against 40.8 hours on placebo. The European trial also showed more pain-free time and fewer phototoxic reactions. Both trials were funded by the manufacturer.

**69 vs 41 h** — median pain-free hours in direct sunlight over six months, afamelanotide vs placebo (US phase 3 trial, 94 adults with EPP) (Langendonk et al., NEJM 2015 · European trial, 74 adults: 6.0 vs 0.8 hours over nine months)  
*Means:* Afamelanotide lets people with EPP spend more time in sunlight without pain. That is approval-grade evidence (A) for one rare disease.  
*Does not mean:* It says nothing about cosmetic tanning, about Melanotan II, or about unsupervised injections. Even in EPP, the US label recommends a full-body skin examination twice a year.

### 10 / 19 · What the case reports show.  
*Safety signals · audio*

The harms of Melanotan II come from case reports and reviews, so they show what can happen without telling you how often. Reported skin effects include darkening and growth of existing moles, crops of new and sometimes atypical naevi, and dark bands in the nails. At least five melanomas have been reported during or after use, each in someone with other risk factors, so causation is unproven. Systemic reports include ischaemic priapism needing surgery and a stimulant-like toxic reaction with rhabdomyolysis, and FDA lists posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome among published serious events.

- Skin: changing moles, eruptive naevi, melanonychia
- Melanoma: reported, causation unproven
- Systemic: priapism, rhabdomyolysis, encephalopathy

Now the harms, and the kind of evidence behind them. Everything here comes from case reports and reviews, which tell you what can happen and leave open how often it does. In the skin, existing moles darken and grow, new moles erupt in crops, some of them atypical under the microscope, and dark bands can appear in the nails. ## Lesson 5.3 · Investigational hair peptides and how to grade them

### 14 / 19 · A Wnt switch and a zinc question.  
*Lesson 5.3 · Concept · audio*

PTD-DBM joins a cell-penetrating peptide to a short decoy sequence that stops the protein CXXC5 from binding Dishevelled. Freed from that brake, Wnt/β-catenin signalling rises, and in mice hair regrew faster. Its developers also found CXXC5 raised in human balding scalp. Zinc thymulin is a thymic peptide that is active only when bound to zinc. How it might act on hair has never been defined, and whether any effect would come from the peptide or the zinc is unknown.

- PTD-DBM: releases a Wnt brake (mouse data)
- Zinc thymulin: a zinc-dependent thymic peptide
- Both: mechanism first, human data absent

<details><summary>Narration script</summary>

Two peptides are sold for hair, and both arrive with better stories than data. PTD-DBM joins a cell-penetrating fragment borrowed from an HIV protein to a short decoy sequence. Inside follicle cells, the decoy keeps a protein called CXXC5 from binding its partner, Dishevelled, and that releases a brake on Wnt signalling, a pathway involved in hair regrowth. In mice, hair regrew faster and follicles formed in healed wounds. The work came from the developer's own group. Zinc thymulin is a thymic hormone that becomes active only when zinc binds to it. How it might help hair has never been defined, and nobody has shown whether any effect would come from the peptide or from the zinc. Hold both as mechanism, and look for the human trial.

</details>

<sub>Sources: Lee SH 2017 · Dardenne M 1982 · Mehta A 2025 · The Peptide Pocket Guide — 2026 Edition · Clinical Reference (seventy-two entries) 2026</sub>

### 15 / 19 · Zero trials in people.  
*The evidence · stat*

As of September 2026, PubMed and ClinicalTrials.gov list no human trial of PTD-DBM, published or registered. Its evidence is one 2017 study in mice and human scalp tissue from the developer's group, whose senior author is affiliated with a biotech company. A 2025 review's summary speaks of 'clinical efficacy', yet the review's own text describes PTD-DBM results in mouse models only.

**0** — human trials of PTD-DBM, published or registered (September 2026) (Lee et al., J Invest Dermatol 2017: mice, plus CXXC5 measured in human scalp tissue)  
*Means:* Whether PTD-DBM regrows human hair is unknown. The Peptide Pocket Guide and this course grade it D.  
*Does not mean:* It does not show that PTD-DBM fails or harms. One open safety question sits alongside: the 2025 review flags that off-target Wnt activation is associated with colorectal and liver cancers, a theoretical concern nobody has tested for this peptide.
