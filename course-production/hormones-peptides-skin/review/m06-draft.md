## Lesson 6.1 · Growth hormone and IGF-1 in skin homeostasis

### 01 / 19 · Real endocrinology, borrowed for marketing.  
*Module 06 · The growth-hormone axis and the skin · title*

Growth hormone and IGF-1 genuinely shape skin, and one peptide on this axis has approval-grade trials. Most of what patients are offered has much less behind it. In three lessons you will separate the physiology from the promise, the approved from the anecdotal, and the side-effects you can see from the risks nobody has measured.

- 6.1 Growth hormone and IGF-1 in the skin
- 6.2 GHRH analogues and secretagogues: what stands behind each
- 6.3 Signs of excess, oedema and mitogenic risk

### 02 / 19 · Two hormones, two layers of skin.  
*Lesson 6.1 · Mechanism · audio*

Growth hormone acts mostly in the dermis. Its receptor is expressed more by fibroblasts than by keratinocytes, and it increases fibroblast numbers and supports collagen synthesis. IGF-1, made locally by fibroblasts and melanocytes, reaches keratinocytes through the IGF-1 receptor and makes them divide. Human hair follicles even run their own small growth-hormone loop in the laboratory. With age, GH secretion and IGF-1 fall steadily; this is the somatopause.

- GH: dermal fibroblasts and collagen
- IGF-1: keratinocyte proliferation, supplied locally
- Somatopause: GH and IGF-1 fall with age

<details><summary>Narration script</summary>

Two layers of skin listen to two different parts of the same axis. Growth hormone speaks mainly to the dermis. Its receptor is expressed more on fibroblasts than on keratinocytes, and in culture growth hormone increases fibroblast numbers and supports collagen synthesis, sometimes directly and sometimes through IGF-1. The epidermis listens to IGF-1. Keratinocytes carry the IGF-1 receptor, their neighbours in the dermis supply the hormone, and in culture IGF-1 makes keratinocytes divide where growth hormone on its own does nothing. Human hair follicles even keep a small growth-hormone loop running when they are cultured outside the body. With age, growth-hormone pulses and IGF-1 fall steadily, a change called the somatopause. Hold all of this as mechanism. It explains why the axis could matter for skin. Whether changing it helps a patient is a separate question, and the trials answer it.

</details>

### 03 / 19 · A much-cited skin result, read again.  
*Human evidence · stat*

In 1990 a much-cited New England Journal of Medicine study gave growth hormone for six months to 12 healthy men over 60, with nine untreated men for comparison and no placebo. Lean mass rose and fat fell. Skin thickness rose 7.1%, with P = 0.07, short of statistical significance. A small 1996 randomized trial in 28 older men found 15% more collagen laid down in implanted wound tubes on GH, a laboratory surrogate for repair.

**7.1%** — rise in skin thickness in 12 older men given growth hormone for six months (1990) (P = 0.07 · nine untreated men as comparison · no placebo)  
*Means:* A skin result still quoted for growth hormone comes from 12 treated men, without a placebo, and it missed statistical significance. Graded on its design, it is D.  
*Does not mean:* It does not show that GH thins skin or that it fails in true deficiency, and it says nothing about secretagogues. It shows how thin the direct skin evidence for growth hormone in healthy ageing is.

### 05 / 19 · Deficiency is a diagnosis. Decline is ageing.  
*What changes in practice · audio*

In healthy older adults, randomized trials of growth hormone show small shifts in body composition, about 2 kg less fat and 2 kg more lean mass, with more oedema, joint pain, carpal tunnel syndrome and a trend towards diabetes. Strength and endurance change little. The Aesthetic & Regenerative Endocrinology guide grades its advice against treatment A; graded on the trials themselves the evidence is B, and the conclusion is the same. In the US, as of September 2026, knowingly distributing growth hormone for anti-ageing is a federal offence.

- Healthy ageing: small body-composition change, more side-effects
- Grade B on the trials (guide: A), same conclusion
- US: distribution for anti-ageing is a federal offence

**By role**
- *Prescriber:* Prescribe growth hormone only for a confirmed deficiency or another approved indication, usually with an endocrinologist. For a request framed as anti-ageing, document the conversation, the evidence you quoted and the legal position, and decline.
- *Clinical staff:* If a patient mentions growth hormone or 'HGH' for skin or energy, record what they use, where it comes from and any swelling, joint pain or tingling, and bring it to the prescriber.
- *Advisor:* You can explain that growth hormone falls normally with age and that trials in healthy adults show more side-effects than benefit. Anyone who wants to start it needs a prescriber's assessment rather than a product recommendation.

## Lesson 6.2 · GHRH analogues and secretagogues: clinical pharmacology

### 07 / 19 · One axis, four ways in.  
*Lesson 6.2 · Concept · audio*

Somatropin is growth hormone itself. GHRH analogues (sermorelin, tesamorelin, CJC-1295) stimulate the pituitary's GHRH receptor. Ghrelin-receptor agonists (ipamorelin, GHRP-2, GHRP-6 and the oral ibutamoren, MK-677) stimulate GH release through a second pituitary receptor. IGF-1 analogues such as IGF-1 LR3 bypass the pituitary altogether. They are sold as one family, and the next moments take them apart.

- Replace: somatropin
- Stimulate: GHRH analogues and ghrelin-receptor agonists
- Bypass: IGF-1 analogues

<details><summary>Narration script</summary>

Four routes lead into the same axis, and patients rarely know which one they are on. Somatropin is growth hormone itself, made by recombinant technology. GHRH analogues, sermorelin, tesamorelin and CJC-1295, press on the pituitary's releasing-hormone receptor, so the gland releases its own growth hormone in pulses. Ghrelin-receptor agonists, ipamorelin, GHRP-2, GHRP-6 and the oral compound ibutamoren, press on a second pituitary receptor. IGF-1 analogues such as IGF-1 LR3 skip the pituitary and act on the IGF-1 receptor directly. Every route ends at that same receptor, which is why lesson three treats their risks together. Clinics often sell all of these as one family with one promise. Keep them apart, because the trials, the approvals and the side-effects belong to each molecule separately.

</details>

### 08 / 19 · What real peptide evidence looks like.  
*Worked example · tool*

Tesamorelin is the instructive exception on this axis. Both Hormonaly guides grade it A, for one indication. Read the record in order.

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| Trials | Two phase 3 RCTs, 806 patients | Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled and run by the developer, in adults with HIV and excess abdominal fat |
| Result | About 15% less visceral fat than placebo at 26 weeks | Subcutaneous fat unchanged; the reduction held to 52 weeks on continued treatment |
| US status (label, August 2026) | FDA-approved since 2010, for one use | Reduction of excess abdominal fat in adults with HIV-associated lipodystrophy. The label says it is not for weight loss and that long-term cardiovascular safety has not been established. |
| Safety, in its own label | IGF-1 above +2 SD in 47% at 26 weeks | Diabetes-range HbA1c in 5% against 1% on placebo; fluid retention; contraindicated in active malignancy |
| Grade | A, for HIV-associated abdominal fat only | No trial has tested skin, anti-ageing or general body shaping |

**Callout:** A GRADE A travels with its indication. The molecule does not carry it anywhere else.

### 09 / 19 · Zero skin trials.  
*The evidence · stat*

A PubMed search in September 2026 found no randomized trial with a skin outcome for tesamorelin, CJC-1295, ipamorelin or IGF-1 LR3. The nearest is a 1997 study of a GHRH(1-29) analogue related to sermorelin, in 19 older adults. Skin thickness measured by caliper rose after 16 weeks, compared with a placebo lead-in rather than a parallel control group.

**0** — randomized trials with a skin outcome for tesamorelin, CJC-1295, ipamorelin or IGF-1 LR3 (PubMed, September 2026) (Closest: a 1997 single-blind study of a GHRH(1-29) analogue, 19 older adults, skin thickness by caliper)  
*Means:* Every skin promise made for these peptides rests on mechanism and anecdote. The nearest data are one small study without a parallel control group (D).  
*Does not mean:* It does not show these peptides harm skin or do nothing for it. It shows the question has never been properly tested, and a clinic brochure cannot fill that gap.

## Lesson 6.3 · GH excess, oedema and mitogenic risk

### 14 / 19 · What too much growth hormone looks like.  
*Lesson 6.3 · Recognition · audio*

Sustained GH excess changes skin in ways you can see. In acromegaly, glycosaminoglycan deposits and oedema make the face, hands and feet puffy and thick; the skin turns oily with large pores; sweating increases; skin tags and acanthosis nigricans appear. Drugs on this axis bring their own signs: fluid retention with swelling, joint pain, tingling and carpal tunnel symptoms. The Endocrine Society recommends measuring IGF-1 when typical acral and facial features are present.

- Soft tissue: puffy, thickened face, hands and feet
- Skin: oily, sweaty, skin tags, acanthosis nigricans
- On treatment: swelling, joint pain, tingling

<details><summary>Narration script</summary>

Excess growth hormone leaves visible marks on skin and soft tissue, and noticing them early matters. In acromegaly, glycosaminoglycan deposits and oedema thicken the face, hands and feet, so features look puffy and coarse. The skin turns oily with large pores, sweating increases, and skin tags and acanthosis nigricans appear. Hands and feet enlarge slowly, and the jaw widens. Drugs on this axis bring their own signs, usually transient, with swelling, joint pain, tingling fingers and carpal tunnel symptoms. On somatropin, the label asks for existing moles to be watched for growth or malignant change. The Endocrine Society recommends measuring IGF-1 when typical acral and facial features are present, and suggests it when several associated conditions cluster. For most of you the job is recognition and referral, and the diagnosis belongs to endocrinology.

</details>

### 15 / 19 · Does raising IGF-1 cause cancer?  
*Four registers · tool*

The honest answer has four parts, and each has its own source. Keep them apart, or a mechanism will be heard as a measured risk.

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Plausible | IGF-1 receptor signalling drives cell proliferation and survival, which gives any IGF-1-raising product a theoretical concern |
| Human observation | Modest associations | In large studies, higher natural IGF-1 goes with slightly higher risks of prostate, breast, colorectal and thyroid cancer; reverse causation is not excluded. In one Ecuadorian cohort, people with GH-receptor deficiency had almost no cancer. |
| Acromegaly | Slightly raised, inconsistently | Pooled studies show about 1.5 times the expected cancer incidence, with the highest estimates from small single-centre series; a Danish nationwide cohort found no clear rise |
| GH replacement | Largely reassuring | No clear raised risk in 23,984 GH-treated children with growth failure and no other disease; a 2022 consensus found no link with primary tumours or recurrence in survivors |
| Open question | Unmeasured | No long-term cancer data exist for secretagogues or IGF-1 LR3 used for ageing or skin |

**Callout:** A plausible mechanism plus a modest association is a reason for caution and consent. It is a different thing from a measured risk.

### 16 / 19 · Half the patients on the approved one.  
*The evidence · stat*

Tesamorelin is the best-studied peptide on this axis. In its pivotal trials, 47% of treated patients had IGF-1 more than two standard deviations above the mean at 26 weeks, and 36% more than three. Its label states that the effects of prolonged IGF-1 elevation are unknown. For unapproved secretagogues bought for skin, nobody has published the equivalent figure.

**47%** — of tesamorelin-treated patients with IGF-1 above +2 SD at 26 weeks in its pivotal trials (36% above +3 SD · label: effects of prolonged elevation unknown)  
*Means:* Pushing IGF-1 above the normal range is common even with an approved GHRH analogue used as labelled, in monitored trials.  
*Does not mean:* It does not show that tesamorelin causes cancer, and it is no target to aim for. It shows why sustained, unmonitored use of any IGF-1-raising product is an open safety question.
